# Truco benchmark — rule ambiguities and resolutions

The rules document is the source of truth. The items below are places where it is
silent, or where two readings are possible. Each has the resolution implemented by
the engine (`truco/`) and the orchestrator (`runner/`). All are flagged for review;
changing a resolution means changing the engine and its golden test.

| # | Topic | Ambiguity | Resolution implemented |
|---|-------|-----------|------------------------|
| 1 | Fold during a pending raise | §8 says folding while responding "counts as declining"; §13 lists only ACCEPT / DECLINE / RAISE as legal responses. What does the caller score? | When a raise is pending the legal set is exactly `ACCEPT`, `DECLINE`, `RAISE`; `DECLINE` *is* the fold. The calling team scores the stake as it stood immediately before the pending call. In a raise-back chain the implicit accept has already happened, so declining a raise-back SEIS pays 3, declining NOVE pays 6, etc. (matches the §7.3 table). |
| 2 | Talk length | §11: "at most 200 characters". Is a longer line illegal, truncated, or dropped? | Talk is never grounds for illegality. Text longer than 200 characters is truncated to 200 and a `talk_truncated` event is recorded with the original length. Characters are counted as Unicode code points after stripping surrounding whitespace. |
| 3 | Mão de onze decider for N > 2 | §9: "That team's mão-side player then chooses". If the team at 11 does not contain the mão, which player is "mão-side"? | The first player of the team at 11 in play order starting from the mão. With N=4: the mão if their team has 11, otherwise the seat after the mão. |
| 4 | Persistence of partner-card knowledge in mão de onze | §12: partner's hand is visible "only during a mão de onze decision". Prompts are stateless, so a literal reading would erase what the players legitimately saw. | During the decision, `observation()` exposes `partner_hand` to **both** members of the team at 11 (the rules say they see each other's cards). After `MAO_PLAY`, the observation for both members carries `partner_hand_at_deal` for the rest of that hand only, which is the "memory" of what was shown. Never exposed to opponents; never carried into the next hand. The isolation test allows exactly this exposure. |
| 5 | Position reuse in mão de ferro | §9: cards "are played by position". Can a position be named twice? What do the positions refer to after a card is played? | Positions 1–3 refer to the dealt order and never shift. A position that has been played is removed from the legal list, so `PLAY 2` twice is illegal on the second attempt. |
| 6 | Pardas in mão de ferro | §9 says the hand is "otherwise resolved normally". | §6 applies unchanged, including "all three pardas → mão's team wins". Because both teams are at 11 the hand winner wins the match. |
| 7 | Same-team tie for the strongest card | §5 says such a tie is not a parda and the team wins, but §5 also says the next leader is "the player who played the winning card" — there are two. | The next leader is the earlier of the tied players in play order (the first one to have shown the winning strength). |
| 8 | Responder in a raise-back chain | §7.3: "the responding team's player who is next to act in seat order". After a raise-back, who responds for the original callers? | The responder is always seat `(last_raiser + 1) mod N`, which is always an opponent of the last raiser (seats alternate teams). This reproduces Example C exactly (1 calls, 2 raises back, 3 responds). After the chain ends in an accept, the seat whose turn was interrupted plays. |
| 9 | Implicit accept on raise-back | §7.3: a raise back "counts as accepting and immediately calling the next step". When does the right to raise move? | The stake is first set to the pending amount and the right moves to the raising-back team; then the new call is created. If the new call is then accepted the right moves again to the accepting team. If declined, the raising-back team scores the amount they implicitly accepted. |
| 10 | When a raise may be called | §7.2: "only on their own turn, before playing a card". Can the same player raise twice in one turn if the first is accepted? | No: after an accept the right belongs to the accepting team, so the caller's team cannot raise until the opponents have raised and been accepted. A player who has already played a card in the current trick cannot raise until their next turn. |
| 11 | Mão de onze with N = 2 | "The players of that team see each other's cards" is vacuous. | The single player of the team at 11 decides; no extra visibility. |
| 12 | Precedence of ferro over onze | Both are "checked at the start of every hand". | If both teams have 11, mão de ferro applies. Otherwise if exactly one team has 11, mão de onze applies. |
| 13 | Forfeit bookkeeping | §9: on forfeit "the opposing team scores 1 point". Does the dealer rotate? Are cards revealed? | The hand counts as played for dealing purposes (dealer rotates). No cards are revealed to anyone outside the team at 11. The event log (not any observation) still records the full deal. |
| 14 | Folding in mão de onze / mão de ferro | §9 says folding "remains allowed" in mão de onze; §9 is silent for ferro but §13 lists FOLD for ferro turns. | FOLD is legal in both. Opponents score the fixed stake (3 or 1). In ferro a fold therefore ends the match. |
| 15 | Duplicate matches | "Same deal sequence, team assignments swapped across seats." | The deal for hand *k* is derived from `(seed, k)` only, so the deal sequence never depends on how earlier hands were played. The random-action fallback uses a separate seed-derived stream. The runner implements a duplicate by swapping which model configuration occupies the even and odd seats; the engine is unchanged. |
| 16 | Illegal-action protocol | §13: shown the legal list "once more and may answer again". Is the second prompt a continuation or a fresh prompt? What if a response contains several `Final Answer:` lines? | The re-prompt is a fresh single-turn prompt containing the full observation, the legal list, and the model's previous response quoted verbatim (the harness's `RETHINK_WITH_ENV_UNPARSABLE` pattern). The **last** `Final Answer:` occurrence is the one parsed (harness convention). A response that soft-matches exactly one legal action is legal; anything else is illegal. |
| 17 | Talk attached to illegal replies | Should table talk from a reply that was illegal still be heard? | No. Only the talk from the reply that produced the applied action is recorded. When the action is a random fallback, no talk is recorded. |
| 18 | Match end check | §10: "at the end of a hand". Do forfeit points count? | Yes. Any hand-ending event (tricks, decline, fold, forfeit) triggers the check. |
| 19 | Previous hands' plays | §12 lists "every card played so far this hand". | Previous hands are summarized by their public results (winner, points, how it ended, final stake). Cards from previous hands are not shown. Unplayed cards are never revealed, not even at hand end. |
| 20 | Card and action spelling | §13 uses `PLAY <card>`. Which spelling is canonical? | Canonical action strings use Unicode suits: `PLAY Q♠`. The soft parser accepts ASCII forms (`QS`, `Q of spades`, `Qs`) and case/whitespace variants, mapping them to exactly one legal action. |
| 21 | Table talk visibility across hands | §12: "all table talk so far". | The observation carries every talk line of the match (with hand and trick index). The prompt renderer shows all talk of the current hand and the last 10 lines from earlier hands. |
| 22 | Terminal actor id | `current_actor()` must return an int even when no decision is pending. | `-4` (`TERMINAL_PLAYER`, the OpenSpiel `PlayerId.TERMINAL` value) when the match is over. |
| 23 | Tricks after a same-team tie count as wins | Follows from §5. | Yes: a same-team tie is a win for that team, with the earlier player leading next. |
| 24 | Stake shown while a raise is pending | The "current stake" (§12) is the accepted stake, not the offered one. | `stake` in the observation is the accepted stake; `pending_call` carries the offered value. Folding is not offered while a call is pending (see #1). |
